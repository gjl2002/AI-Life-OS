# Content Empathy Radar

Use this reference before selecting the article angle, opening, title direction, and reader-flow structure. It helps the article enter the reader's emotional scene before explaining or converting.

Do not use it as proof by itself. Apply it to verified reader material, user-avatar insights, comments, private-domain feedback, benchmark reactions, product evidence, or the user's own observed scenes.

## Working Table

For the chosen reader, build a compact working table:

```markdown
| Reader evidence | Emotion entry | What the reader is really protecting / wanting | Article entry angle | Risk of triggering defense |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
```

Keep original reader wording when available. Label interpretations as fact, insight, or hypothesis.

## Emotion To Article Jobs

### 不爽 -> opening conflict

Use when readers feel stuck, tired, annoyed, or disappointed.

Ask:
- What feels "technically correct but useless" to the reader?
- What repeated friction makes the topic worth reading now?
- What old explanation should the opening challenge?

Best for:
- pain-point teardown.
- strong-judgment opinion.
- product conversion article.

### 焦虑 -> reader question

Use when readers are comparing, collecting, fearing missed opportunities, or unable to decide.

Ask:
- What future risk is the reader imagining?
- What question is looping in their head?
- What uncertainty can the article organize or reduce?

Best for:
- trend judgment.
- method article.
- title and first 3 paragraphs.

### 恐惧 -> deep pain

Use when the reader fears failure, being left behind, wasting effort, losing identity, or exposing incompetence.

Ask:
- What outcome would hurt if nothing changes?
- What does the reader rarely say out loud?
- What must be handled with proof, not slogans?

Best for:
- core argument.
- stakes section.
- product bridge.

### 羞耻 -> gentle naming

Use when readers may feel behind, chaotic, lazy, incapable, or embarrassed.

Ask:
- What wording would make the reader close the article?
- How can the article name the problem without humiliating the reader?
- Where should first-person confession or personal scene soften the claim?

Best for:
- opening scene.
- transition into method.
- CTA or diagnostic invitation.

### 防御 -> avoid education mode

Use when the article could sound like lecturing, selling, or "I know better than you."

Ask:
- Which paragraph tries to educate before entering the reader's scene?
- Which claim sounds too certain for the evidence?
- Where should the article reduce pressure or use a smaller next step?

Best for:
- reader-flow pass.
- conversion path.
- AI-flavor cleanup.

### 爽 / 愉悦 -> release and ending

Use when the article can show relief, clarity, identity alignment, or a first concrete win.

Ask:
- What tension does the method release?
- What first small result can the reader imagine owning?
- What final sentence returns dignity or possibility to the reader?

Best for:
- solution reveal.
- product mechanism.
- ending.

## Angle Selection

Before drafting, decide:

- emotion entry: 不爽 / 焦虑 / 恐惧 / 羞耻 / 防御 / 爽 / 愉悦.
- article promise: what confusion or pressure will be reduced.
- reader risk: what expression may make the reader defensive.
- conversion bridge: whether the article should mention a product, invite diagnosis, or avoid conversion.

If the topic is reasonable but has no clear emotion entry, gather more reader evidence or write it as a light observation instead of a full article.

## Guardrails

- Do not manufacture anxiety to make an article feel stronger.
- Do not mistake the user's own excitement for reader demand.
- Do not start with abstract definitions when a concrete emotional scene is available.
- Do not call a reader lazy, low cognition, chaotic, or backward.
- Do not use benchmark emotion if the user's audience evidence points elsewhere.
