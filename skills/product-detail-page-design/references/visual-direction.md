# Adaptable Visual Direction

Use the user's own brand system, product positioning, and supplied reference images as the primary visual authority.

## Priority

1. User-supplied brand guide, product assets, campaign brief, and approved examples.
2. Visual patterns derived from the target audience, promise, product form, and sales architecture.
3. The neutral fallback direction below only when no stronger direction exists.

Do not reuse another product's brand name, proprietary screenshot, exact composition, watermark, logo, personal photo, course data, price, testimonial, or proof.

## Neutral Fallback

For technology, AI, knowledge-system, or digital-product offers, a restrained premium data-tech direction may use:

- deep charcoal or near-black background;
- off-white primary type;
- cool-gray borders and fine grid lines;
- one controlled warm accent and one controlled cool accent;
- modular rounded cards;
- product-relevant diagrams or abstract 3D forms;
- generous negative space;
- compact evidence blocks;
- large result-oriented headings;
- subtle technical labels and restrained glow.

This is a fallback, not a mandatory house style. Lifestyle, education, consulting, cultural, or personal-service offers may need a warmer or more editorial direction.

## Adaptation Card

Before generating pages, state the brand/product, target user, core promise, visual assets that must be preserved, forbidden elements, colors, typography, image language, and proof treatment.

## Safety

- Never infer a user's logo, portrait, customer screenshot, testimonial, metrics, or brand colors.
- Do not display private Notion pages, personal records, contact details, order data, or unpublished product information unless the user explicitly supplies and authorizes them.
- Keep third-party references structural: learn hierarchy, rhythm, density, and visual mechanism without copying logos, text, screenshots, or exact layouts.
