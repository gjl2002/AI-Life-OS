# Conversion Empathy Radar

Use this reference before writing sales-page architecture and page copy. It translates user emotion into conversion jobs, not generic empathy.

Do not use this as evidence by itself. Apply it only to sourced user material, product notes, objections, testimonials, comments, consultation records, FAQs, or confirmed avatar insights.

## Conversion Radar Table

For important buyer evidence, build this working table:

```markdown
| Buyer evidence | Emotion entry | Hidden need / fear | Conversion friction | Page job | Copy / proof response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
```

Mark any interpretation as `confirmed`, `inferred`, or `missing`.

## Emotion To Page Jobs

### 恐惧 -> pain and urgency

Ask:
- What bad outcome does the buyer want to avoid?
- What happens if they delay?
- What cost feels most real: money, time, identity, career, relationship, dignity, or opportunity?

Use in:
- cognitive conflict.
- failed path.
- why-now explanation.
- guarantee, proof, FAQ, or onboarding promise.

Avoid exaggerated fear selling. If the source only shows mild inconvenience, do not write it as a deep fear.

### 焦虑 -> decision support

Ask:
- What uncertainty blocks the buyer from deciding?
- What comparison, criteria, or sequence would calm the decision?
- What does the buyer need to understand before price appears?

Use in:
- mechanism diagram.
- suitability section.
- step-by-step implementation path.
- FAQ and risk reversal.

### 防御 -> trust and resistance handling

Ask:
- What makes the buyer skeptical?
- Are they protecting money, time, autonomy, privacy, status, or self-image?
- Which promise sounds too good, too vague, too pushy, or too similar to past bad offers?

Use in:
- proof beside claims.
- transparent limitations.
- "not for you if" section.
- price/terms/CTA wording.

Never try to crush defense with louder claims. Reduce pressure, add proof, narrow the promise, or make the next step smaller.

### 羞耻 -> dignity protection

Ask:
- What problem does the buyer not want to expose?
- Would direct wording make them feel blamed, behind, stupid, lazy, or chaotic?
- How can the page name the problem without attacking the person?

Use in:
- audience self-selection.
- before/after framing.
- onboarding and support promise.
- private or low-exposure CTA.

### 不爽 -> friction and failed path

Ask:
- What repeatedly annoys the buyer?
- Which previous method was technically usable but emotionally unrewarding?
- What small friction makes the old path collapse?

Use in:
- failed alternatives.
- product mechanism.
- feature-to-result translation.
- "why previous approach did not compound."

### 爽 -> activation and result reveal

Ask:
- What long-tightened need gets released when the product works?
- What first win makes the buyer feel "finally"?
- Which result is vivid enough to anchor the hero or outcome section?

Use in:
- hero promise.
- first deliverable.
- outcome page.
- after-purchase activation path.

Use `naming-word-of-mouth.md` with this emotion radar when the page needs a memorable public name or referral sentence:

- Hero: the product name should be sayable and should summon the buyer's desired future or remembered problem.
- Proof: testimonials should support the口碑句, not scatter into unrelated praise.
- Outcomes: the page should make clear what over-delivered moment would make users recommend it.
- CTA: the final action should not introduce a colder or more internal name than the hero.

Do not promise guaranteed transformation. Show the concrete artifact, behavior loop, or owned system the buyer can obtain.

### 愤怒 -> boundary repair

Ask:
- What past offer, platform, seller, or process violated the buyer's boundary?
- What felt unfair, manipulative, exposed, or disrespectful?
- What boundary must this offer explicitly protect?

Use in:
- trust section.
- refund/terms.
- privacy and delivery rules.
- "what this is not" section.

## Placement In Sales Architecture

Use the radar to sharpen the 11-module architecture:

- Hero: name the desired result and first release moment.
- Cognitive conflict: break the old explanation without blaming the buyer.
- Failed path: name real frustrations and failed attempts.
- Unique mechanism: show how the product reduces fear, uncertainty, or friction.
- Proof: answer defenses with sourced evidence.
- Audience: write desire plus obstacle, not demographic labels.
- Outcomes: translate features into owned results.
- Blueprint/path: lower anxiety through sequence.
- Bundle/price/CTA: reduce ambiguity and pressure.

## Copy Guardrails

- Do not turn every pain into fear.
- Do not shame the buyer for needing help.
- Do not claim "用户一定会..." unless evidence supports it.
- Do not use role assumptions such as 学员应该自律 or 创业者应该愿意折腾.
- Prefer: "你不是不努力，而是缺少..." only when evidence supports that interpretation.
- When evidence is thin, write `[待确认]` or use cautious wording.
