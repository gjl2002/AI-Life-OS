# Article Structure Library

Use this file after reading the user's topic or draft. Choose the article structure by task, not by habit.

## Selection Rule

First decide:
- What does this article need to accomplish?
- Which type is primary?
- Is there a secondary type?
- Which structure would be wrong for this topic?

## Six Article Types

| Type | Main job | Best for | External evidence | Product bridge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong-judgment opinion | Create recognition and spread | Growth/follower articles | Optional | Light |
| Pain-point teardown | Make readers feel understood | User education | Not required | Light |
| Trend judgment | Build momentum and authority | Big stage-setting articles | Recommended | Optional |
| Personal story | Build trust and realness | Long-term relationship | Not required | Light |
| Method list | Prove method and delivery ability | Pre-conversion education | Optional | Stronger |
| Product conversion | Sell or drive private-domain action | Launch/sales period | Optional | Required |

## 1. Strong-Judgment Opinion

Use when the article needs to overturn a common belief.

Structure:
1. Contrarian judgment.
2. Concrete phenomenon that makes readers nod.
3. First drilldown: not A, not B, the real problem is C.
4. User's real observation: why I dare say this.
5. Optional external trend/proof.
6. Core judgment elevation.
7. One small action.
8. Light product bridge.
9. Closing echo.

Good for:
- AI use misunderstandings.
- Growth misunderstandings.
- Tool dependency.
- Knowledge accumulation without action.

## 2. Pain-Point Teardown

Use when the article needs to make the reader feel seen.

Structure:
1. Pain judgment.
2. Concrete daily state.
3. Ask why this keeps happening.
4. Root cause.
5. Analogy or simple mechanism.
6. User observation or user case.
7. Solution path.
8. 1-3 small actions.
9. Light product bridge.

Good for:
- Goals scatter after planning.
- Tools make people more anxious.
- AI conversations are inspiring but do not become action.

## 3. Trend Judgment

Use when the article needs outside momentum.

Structure:
1. Trend/hot phenomenon.
2. Strong trend judgment.
3. Public data, company example, or authority.
4. What ordinary people misunderstand.
5. What will become scarce.
6. User's judgment.
7. What ordinary people should do.
8. Method or product bridge.

Use web search when the trend or data must be current.

## 4. Personal Story

Use when the article needs trust and emotional truth.

Structure:
1. Real scene.
2. My misunderstanding then.
3. Turning point.
4. What I did.
5. Result or change.
6. Judgment extracted from the story.
7. Return to the reader.
8. Light method/product bridge.

Only use first person when the story is verified from the user or Notion sources.

## 5. Method List

Use when the article needs to prove the user has a usable method.

Structure:
1. Specific problem.
2. Common wrong way.
3. Correct principle.
4. 3-5 steps.
5. Example for each step.
6. What changes after doing it.
7. Product/template/community bridge.

Avoid turning this into a course handout. Each method point should have one sharp angle.

## 6. Product Conversion

Use when the article needs to sell, invite consultation, or drive private-domain action.

Structure:
1. Result or credibility opening.
2. Why I made this product.
3. Why the reader needs it.
4. Product vision.
5. Case/feedback evidence.
6. Product structure and benefits.
7. Fit / not fit.
8. Price or value anchor if available.
9. Action call.

Keep claims grounded in resolved `product`, `user_feedback`, `client_communication`, and other verified cloud Notion evidence.
