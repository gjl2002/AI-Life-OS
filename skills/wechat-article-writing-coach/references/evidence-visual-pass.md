# Evidence Visual Pass

Use this when a WeChat article would benefit from real business evidence visuals inside the body. This is not a cover-image module, not a quote-card module, and not a decorative illustration module.

The goal is to help the reader see that the article's judgment is backed by the user's real system, training-camp work, product work, workflow, user feedback, or working context.

## Core Standard

Prefer real business visuals over abstract illustrations.

Use visuals only when they make the article more credible, concrete, or easier to understand. Do not add images for decoration.

When a real screenshot already proves a structure, workflow, product state, or usage fact, it should replace duplicate explanatory prose. The text should tell the reader what to notice and why it matters, not inventory everything visible in the image.

Default priority:
1. Real product screenshots.
2. Real Notion / personal-system / workspace screenshots.
3. Real workflow screenshots.
4. Real user feedback or private-domain evidence screenshots with sensitive information masked.
5. Real delivery, diagnosis, consultation, or project-material screenshots.
6. Simple schematic or conceptual visuals only when no real evidence visual exists.

## When To Add Evidence Visuals

Add 1-3 inline evidence visual placeholders when one of these appears in the article:
- the user claims there is a real system, workflow, product, database, or method behind the article
- the article explains a concept that becomes clearer when readers see the actual workspace
- the article mentions Notion, 创作大脑, AI workflow, review system, training-camp material, product design, or private-domain service
- the article needs to shift from "I am making a point" to "I am actually doing this"
- a user feedback, case, or delivery proof would strengthen trust

Do not add evidence visuals when:
- the article is mainly a private personal story and visuals would expose sensitive material
- the article is short and the visual would interrupt momentum
- no real visual can support the claim and a decorative image would weaken trust
- the user asks for text only

## Inline Placeholder Rule

Evidence visuals should appear inside the article body at the exact reading moment where they help.

Use this placeholder format in the draft:

```markdown
【图 1：创作大脑真实截图】
```

The placeholder should appear between paragraphs, not only in a final recommendation section.

Good insertion moments:
- after explaining a real system or database
- after a personal scene that needs proof of accumulated context
- before or after a framework that came from the user's actual workflow
- after mentioning Notion, 创作大脑, a training-camp module, or a delivery process
- before the product bridge when the reader needs to see that the product is real

Do not place visuals at the very end unless the ending itself needs proof. Evidence visuals are part of the reading path, not a postscript.

## Final Visual Notes

After the article, provide concrete visual instructions only for the inline placeholders that were inserted.

Use this format:

```markdown
业务证据配图建议：

【图 1：创作大脑真实截图】
位置：讲到“上下文不是聊天记录，而是长期沉淀”之后
素材：Notion 创作大脑 / 内容库 / 选题库截图
画面处理：隐藏敏感字段，保留页面结构、标题、标签、状态
作用：证明我不是在讲概念，而是真的把内容上下文系统化了
```

Each visual note must include:
- visual title matching the inline placeholder
- insertion position
- real material to capture
- masking or privacy treatment
- article function

## Evidence Visual Types

## Live Commercial Naming Rule

Real business visuals are for proof, not premature named-product promotion.

- Align inline placeholders, captions, CTA text, and visual notes with current `commercial_positioning`, relevant `product` records, and the user's authorized campaign boundary.
- If the screenshot exposes a name outside that boundary, recommend cropping, masking, or a truthful category-level caption derived from the live source.
- A screenshot can show the business; the surrounding copy should still serve the article's reader problem.

### Product Or Training-Camp Screenshot

Use when the article mentions AI task flow, review, goal tracking, training-camp material, or product capability.

Possible materials:
- goal breakdown page
- task continuation page
- review summary page
- AI memory or context page
- training-camp outline, lesson draft, diagnostic table, or delivery material

Function:
- show product reality
- make the abstract product promise visible
- reduce the feeling of hard selling

### System Screenshot

Use when the article mentions context, accumulation, content assets, Notion, second brain, or 创作大脑.

Possible materials:
- 创作大脑 / 内容 database
- topic database
- user profile database
- commercial positioning page
- review system page
- knowledge model page

Function:
- show that the user has a real operating system
- turn an abstract concept into a visible workspace
- prove that the method is practiced, not invented for the article

### Workflow Screenshot

Use when the article explains a process or transition from chaos to structure.

Possible materials:
- raw notes to structured article workflow
- AI conversation to Notion entry
- review to next action
- user feedback to product improvement
- idea to article outline

Function:
- show cause and effect
- help readers understand the method
- make the process feel reproducible

### Feedback Screenshot

Use when the article needs trust, social proof, or user pain evidence.

Possible materials:
- WeChat feedback
- consultation question
- private-domain objection
- user result
- product experience comment

Privacy treatment:
- mask names, avatars, phone numbers, prices, and private details
- preserve the real question, pain, result, or praise
- avoid exaggerating one feedback screenshot into broad proof

### Delivery Evidence

Use when the article involves consulting, diagnosis, service, or transformation.

Possible materials:
- diagnosis sheet
- consultation prep document
- delivery document
- analysis table
- before/after structure

Function:
- show service depth
- make the offer credible
- connect article judgment to real delivery

## Selection Rules

For each full article, decide whether evidence visuals are needed.

If needed, select 1-3 visuals. Two visuals are usually enough.

For each candidate visual, ask:
- Does this image prove or clarify a claim in the article?
- Is this a real business/product/workflow/user-evidence image?
- Can sensitive information be safely hidden?
- Does this image appear at the right reading moment?
- Would the article be weaker without it?
- Which paragraph can be shortened or deleted because this image now carries the proof?

If the answer is no, do not add the image.

## Anti-Patterns

Do not default to:
- cover images
- golden sentence images
- decorative flowcharts
- generic AI illustrations
- stock-style office pictures
- abstract metaphor pictures
- visuals that only repeat the paragraph

Do not insert images only because the article feels long. Insert them because the reader needs visible proof.

Do not keep a full prose explanation and then add a screenshot that says the same thing. If the screenshot is legible and trustworthy, keep only the causal interpretation or viewing cue that the image cannot supply by itself.

## Pass Criteria

When evidence visuals are used:
- every inline placeholder has a matching final visual note
- every visual note points to a real screenshot or real material source
- every visual has a clear article function
- every evidence visual either replaces redundant prose or contributes proof the prose cannot provide
- privacy treatment is specified when user, client, or private-domain material appears
- the visual appears inside the article at the moment it supports, not only in the final notes

When evidence visuals are not used:
- the article can remain text-only without losing credibility or clarity
- no decorative visual is recommended as filler
